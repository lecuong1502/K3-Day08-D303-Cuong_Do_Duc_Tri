Accommodation Advice for
International Students in Vietnam

Finding a comfortable and stable place to live is one of the most important steps as you settle into life in

Vietnam. Whether you're staying short-term or looking for a long-term home, it's essential to understand

how renting works here — from contacting real estate agents to ending a lease.

Step 1: Looking & applying for rental property.

Finding a place to live in Vietnam usually involves working with a real estate agent — a third-party who

helps connect you with available apartments or shared homes. Most agents speak basic English and are

familiar with working with international students.

Where to Search

You can begin your search through:

•  RMIT’s Accommodation Suggestion List – trusted and student-friendly options

•  Google – search terms like “Apartment for rent in District 7” or “Studio near RMIT”

•  Facebook Groups – look for expat or housing communities (e.g., “Apartments for Rent in

HCMC”)

How to Contact Real Estate Agents

Once you find a listing or agency:

•  Clearly explain your budget, preferred location (e.g., District 7), length of stay, and

any specific requirements (furnished/unfurnished; utilities included, etc.)

•  Ask whether the agent charges any service fees or commission (some do, some don’t)

•  Request photos or videos of the property if you can't visit in person

💡💡Pro tip: Most agents will use mobile apps like Zalo, WhatsApp

Step 2: Rental Contract (leases)

A contract is an agreement between you and the rental provider about what each of you will do, or
not do, while you are renting the property.

Lease Duration

•  The standard lease in Vietnam is usually 6–12 months

•  However, it is possible to negotiate a shorter lease (e.g., 3–4 months), especially if you're

staying for a short study period

💡💡Pro tip: Be upfront about your stay duration when talking to agents or landlords

What to Look for in the Contract

Before signing anything, ensure the contract clearly includes:

•  Rental price (monthly rent amount and due date)

•  Lease duration (start and end dates)

•  Security deposit terms (how much, and move-out conditions under what conditions it will

be refunded)

•  Maintenance responsibilities (what you fix vs. what the landlord handles)

•  Utilities (what’s included in the rent, and what’s not)

•  Early termination clause (Notice period for ending the contract (often 30 days))

Deposit & Advance Payment

Once the contract is agreed upon, you’ll typically be asked to pay:

•  A security deposit of 1–2 months' rent (varies by property and lease length)

•  Sometimes 1 month’s rent in advance as a commitment

•  Make sure to get a written receipt for all payments — this is important for your records and

for when you move out

💡💡Pro tip: Don’t hand over money unless you’ve reviewed and signed the contract.

Step 3: Starting Tenancy

Residence Registration – Don’t Skip This Step!

All international students must have their residence registered with the local ward police. Failure to

register can cause issues with visa renewals or legal paperwork — so double-check that this step is

completed after you move in.

Usually, landlords or building management will handle this process for you. But do not assume it’s done

— always confirm and follow up.

They will typically need:

•  A copy of valid passport & visa

•  Your rental contract

Ask for signed confirmation by landlord once the registration is approved by local authorization.

Step 4: Ending Tenancy

To properly end your lease:

•  Notify your landlord in advance (usually 30 days’ notice is required but check your contract for

agreed term)

•  Clean the property and repair any damage

•  Return all keys and access cards

•  Be present for a final inspection

•  Ensure all utility bills are paid

•  Your deposit will be refunded, minus any damage or unpaid costs

💡💡Pro tip: Living in Shared Housing - Sharing with others is a cost-effective option but requires good
communication.

•  Discuss house rules, bills, rent, and cleaning schedules, guests, and quiet hours

•  Ensure everyone’s name is on the lease, or that there's a written agreement with the head

tenant

•  Be mindful of cultural differences and personal space

Need Help?

If you're unsure or need assistance:

•  Contact the International Student Support Team at RMIT Vietnam via

international.students@rmit.edu.vn

•  Reach out to reliable housing agents listed in our Accommodation Suggestions

•  Do not pay deposits or rent until you've verified the property and signed an agreement!
